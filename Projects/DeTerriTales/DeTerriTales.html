DeTerriTales
A Critical Cartography Practice

DeTerriTales is an experimental, interactive cartography that resists the map’s claim to stability, tracing Weimar instead as a contingent and shifting network of relations.

DeTerriTales, Link to the Video.

“The map is open and connectable in all of its dimensions; it is detachable, reversible, susceptible to constant modification. It can be torn, reversed, adapted to any kind of mounting, reworked by an individual, group, or social formation... Unlike the tracing, which always returns to the same, the map has multiple entryways.”

— Deleuze & Guattari, A Thousand Plateaus

Static maps claim territories, but they fail to reveal the ongoing social processes that traverse and transform urban space. This experimental cartographic visualization aims to present Weimar as a rhizomatic assemblage of scenes connected through imagination, moving beyond traditional old-town-centric mapping practices. It seeks to reveal the transformation of the body the city through time and navigation. a map not as a static image asserting territory, but as an ongoing process of deterritorialization.

This project is an interactive visualization of a city, less a map, more a field of unfolding connections. Inspired by Deleuze and Guattari’s notion of the rhizome, it resists fixed centers and linear paths. Instead, it spreads, links, diverges, and returns like memory, like wandering.

Built as a digital collage, the work gathers fragments from different historical layers: the Weimar Republic, National Socialism, the GDR. These moments don’t stack neatly into chronology, but coexist in tension and resonance. Images drift into one another. Boundaries blur. A building is no longer just itself, but a point of convergence, where past and present, here and elsewhere, overlap.

As users explore this space, they move through a terrain shaped by repetition and rupture. Places reappear under new guises; time folds in on itself. The city becomes not a single story, but a network of transformations—half-remembered, half-reassembled.

This is a cartography of becoming, not being.

The project was exhibited as an interactive installation at the Summaery 2025 in Weimar.
